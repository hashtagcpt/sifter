Sifter Methodology

A deep dive into the quantitative models powering Sifter's market screening and risk probability engine.

1. Composite Rank Score

The Sifter engine evaluates the stock universe using a multi-factor composite Rank Score. This score strictly uses a Percentile Rank (0 to 100) cross-sectionally across the entire universe to ensure the scale is fully utilized and resistant to outliers. It combines fundamental value, technical mean-reversion, analyst sentiment, stochastic probabilities, and macro-AI adjustments.

Formula

Raw Score = (Value Score × w_val)
           - (Trend Z-Score × w_trend)
           + (Analyst Buy Count × w_analyst)
           + (Target Probability × w_garch)
           + (AI Macro Score × w_macro)

Rank Score = Percentile_Rank(Raw Score) × 100

Components

  • Value Score: A fundamental metric derived by comparing a stock's P/E, P/B, and EV/EBITDA ratios against standardized neutral baselines. Lower multiples translate to higher (better) scores.
  • Trend Z-Score (Anti-Momentum / Reversal): To capture mean-reversion, we evaluate the asset's z-score relative to its moving average. A negative weight (e.g. w_trend = -1.0) means we favor technically oversold assets, capturing the short-term reversal factor.
  • Analyst Buy Count: A sum of "Buy" and "Strong Buy" recommendations aggregated from Wall Street analysts.
  • Target Probability: The statistical likelihood of reaching the upside target before the downside stop, computed via Monte Carlo simulations (detailed below).
  • AI Macro Score: A sector-wide modifier generated by a local LLM evaluating current macroeconomic conditions.
  • Percentile Ranking: Rather than a linear min-max scale which suffers from clustering, the raw score is ranked against the universe. A score of 95.0 means the stock's composite score is better than 95% of the evaluated universe.
Citation: Asness, C. S., Moskowitz, T. J., & Pedersen, L. H. (2013). Value and Momentum Everywhere. The Journal of Finance, 68(3), 929-985. [Link]
Citation: Fama, E. F., & French, K. R. (2015). A five-factor asset pricing model. Journal of Financial Economics, 116(1), 1-22. [Link]
Citation: Ang, A., Hodrick, R. J., Xing, Y., & Zhang, X. (2006). The Cross-Section of Volatility and Expected Returns. The Journal of Finance, 61(1), 259-299. [Link]

2. Upside and Downside Risk Probabilities

Rather than relying on static standard deviations, Sifter calculates the explicit probability of a trade hitting a defined Upside Target versus hitting a defined Downside Stop over a specific Time Horizon. This is achieved through stochastic modeling and Monte Carlo simulation.

Monte Carlo Simulation Engine

For each asset, the engine projects thousands of potential future price paths over the given horizon. The probabilities are determined by evaluating the trajectory of each path:

  • Probability of Success: The percentage of paths that hit the Target Upside before hitting the Downside Stop (or hit the upside and never hit the stop).
  • Probability of Stop: The percentage of paths that hit the Downside Stop before hitting the Target Upside.

Available Stochastic Models

The path generation relies on different volatility models, primarily focusing on Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (GARCH) to account for volatility clustering and "fat tails" commonly found in financial returns.

  • GBM (Geometric Brownian Motion): Assumes constant volatility and normally distributed returns.
    Standard formula: dS = μS dt + σS dW
  • GARCH (1,1): Models changing variance over time. It predicts future volatility based on past squared returns and past variances.
    Formula: σ²_t = ω + α ε²_{t-1} + β σ²_{t-1}
  • EGARCH / GJR-GARCH / TGARCH: Asymmetric extensions of GARCH that capture the "leverage effect"—the tendency for volatility to rise more following negative returns than positive returns of the same magnitude.
  • Historical Empirical: A non-parametric approach that samples actual rolling overlapping return paths from the asset's historical data.
Citation: Bollerslev, T. (1986). Generalized autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity. Journal of Econometrics, 31(3), 307-327. [Link]
Citation: Engle, R. F. (1982). Autoregressive Conditional Heteroscedasticity with Estimates of the Variance of United Kingdom Inflation. Econometrica, 50(4), 987-1007. [Link]

3. Statistical Arbitrage (Pairs Trading)

The Stat-Arb module evaluates the statistical relationship between two assets to find mean-reverting spreads.

Cointegration & Half-Life

We use the Engle-Granger Two-Step Method to test for cointegration. If the p-value is significant (< 0.05), the two non-stationary price series form a stationary linear combination (the spread). The Hedge Ratio (Beta) determines the position sizing.

The mean-reverting speed of the spread is quantified by estimating the Half-Life using an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process regression.

Citation: Engle, R. F., & Granger, C. W. J. (1987). Co-Integration and Error Correction: Representation, Estimation, and Testing. Econometrica, 55(2), 251-276. [Link]

4. Volatility & Risk Metrics

Sifter utilizes several historical and forward-looking risk metrics to contextualize asset movements and assist with dynamic position sizing or stop-loss placements.

Realized Volatility (RV)

The historical annualized volatility of an asset over a 21-day window.

RV = σ(21-day log returns) × √252

Average True Range (ATR)

A technical indicator measuring market volatility over a 14-day period by decomposing the entire range of an asset price for that period.

TR = max(High - Low, |High - Prev Close|, |Low - Prev Close|)
ATR = 14-day Rolling Mean of TR

Historical Volatility Rank (HV Rank)

The percentile rank of the current 21-day Realized Volatility relative to its range over the past year (252 trading days). A rank of 0 means volatility is at its 52-week low; 100 means it is at its 52-week high.

HV Rank = (Current RV - Min RV252d) / (Max RV252d - Min RV252d) × 100

Current Implied Volatility (IV)

A forward-looking metric derived from the Black-Scholes pricing of the asset's near-term options chain, reflecting the market's forecast of a likely movement in the security's price.

5. Historical Strategy Performance & Bear Market Resilience

To validate the integrity of Sifter's statistical and technical models, it is crucial to examine its performance not just during bull markets, but specifically during systemic drawdowns. The most recent comprehensive stress test of the model includes a 5-year simulation spanning across the 2022 bear market.

5-Year Backtest Results (Including 2022 Bear Market)

  • Total Return: +129.97%
  • Maximum Drawdown: -31.16%

During the 2022 macroeconomic tightening cycle, broader equity markets experienced aggressive, correlated drawdowns. Sifter's anti-momentum / mean-reversion filters successfully identified technically oversold assets, while its strict Statistical Arbitrage & Correlation Thresholds (keeping internal portfolio correlation below 0.4) ensured that capital wasn't concentrated in highly correlated risks.

Configured with a medium-term 120-day holding horizon and monthly rebalancing (every 20 trading days), the strategy blunted the impact of the bear market. The GARCH-modeled probability engine combined with fundamental valuation rankings allowed it to systematically ride medium-term macro rotations, achieving an absolute return of 129.97% (outperforming the SPY benchmark's 81.56%).
